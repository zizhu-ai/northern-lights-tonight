## Usual destinations (does not vanish on a quiet night)

**Oval most nights.** Alaska Interior (Fairbanks area), northern Minnesota, Michigan’s Upper Peninsula, and northern Maine sit closer to the auroral oval. They still have clouds, twilight, and no-show nights. They are the default US places people travel for aurora, not a promise for tonight.

**Event nights only.** Colorado, Ohio, Illinois / Chicago, Indiana, and similar mid-latitude states need a strong storm. Most nights are no. When they go, the useful sky is usually the northern part of the state, away from the biggest light dome — not downtown.

Travel destinations are the first group. Tonight’s comparison is the live table. Do not fly to an event-only state because a friend saw color in Fairbanks.

## How to use a GO or MAYBE

A GO still needs darkness, a north view, and 30–60 minutes. A MAYBE names the obstacle. Clouds mean a darker hill under the same deck does not help. City glow means leaving town might help. Horizon-only means you need a flat north edge, not a south-facing slope.

This page is not a parking-lot list. We will not name unofficial pullouts. Read [how to see](/guides/how-to-see-northern-lights) for posture and [best time](/guides/best-time-to-see-northern-lights) for season. [How we decide](/methodology) is the gate order.

## How the groups are ordered

GO first because that is the only “worth a special trip” bucket. MAYBE second because an obstacle might be solvable (leave town, wait for a hole). NO last because most nights live there. UNKNOWN is a data problem, not a destination.

Empty groups are omitted so a quiet night does not show a hollow GO heading. That is a feature.
